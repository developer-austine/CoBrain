1 · System Overview & Design Philosophy
CoBrain's forecasting engine predicts organisational trajectories — burnout risk, delivery velocity,
cash-flow, churn, and more — across many companies spanning different industries and countries,
using a single conditioned model.
The four load-bearing decisions
Decision 1 — One conditioned model, not one model per tenant
A single sequence model serves every company. Company identity enters through conditioning signals
(industry, geography) rather than separate models. This gives cross-tenant learning — patterns
learned from the whole fleet improve every tenant's forecast — while keeping per-tenant specialisation.

Decision 2 — Three tiers, three distinct mechanisms
Each tier operates differently because each tier is different in nature:
TIER 	MECHANISM 	PRODUCES
1 · Universal 	Sequence encoder 	Base feature trajectory z_t
2 · Industry 	Feature mask + projector 	Feature mask m_ind
3 · Geography Latent projector (static) + known-future channel 	Geo latent e_geo + time-varying geo signals
Decision 3 — Quantile output, always
The model never emits a single number. It emits three quantiles — Bear (P10), Base (P50), Bull (P90) —
so the forecast band communicates honest uncertainty. In volatile markets, band width is itself the
insight.
Decision 4 — Tenant isolation by namespace
Every store is namespaced per tenant. No tenant's raw data or trajectory is ever visible to another.
Isolation is enforced at the data layer, not just the API.
T H E C O R E I N S I G H T
Your three tiers map cleanly onto the three input channels a temporal fusion model consumes natively:
observed (Tier 1 sequences), static (Industry mask + Geography identity), and known-future
(scheduled geography signals). The architecture is the composition of these three.

2 · The Corrected Architecture
This is your uploaded design, corrected into an executable form. Seven fixes were applied. Five
correct the architecture itself; two more (added in version 2) correct the output head and the
conditioning path that makes the three-tier design actually function.
T H E S E V E N F I X E S A P P L I E D
1. z_t is now explicitly a per-timestep sequence tensor [T×d], broadcast at fusion. 2. Industry mask
m_ind gates the encoder INPUT (not its output). 3. Geography split into static latent e_geo + time-varying
known-future g_t. 4. All stores labelled "per-tenant namespace". 5. Fusion engine emits a second head —
interpretability weights. 6. The output head uses the last timestep, never Flatten. 7. The static covariate
encoder initialises the LSTM hidden state.
F I X 6 — W H Y F L AT T E N I S T H E W R O N G H E A D
A common time-series transformer tutorial flattens the [T×d] sequence into one long vector before the
output layer, because it predicts a single scalar. That is wrong here for three reasons: it explodes the
parameter count, it hard-locks seq_len so the lookback window can never vary, and it destroys the
temporal structure the encoder just built. Use the last timestep's hidden state, h[:, -1, :] , and project
it to the full horizon.
F I X 7 — T H E C O N D I T I O N I N G PAT H T H AT M A K E S T H E T I E R S R E A L
The static covariate encoder must produce four context vectors — c_h and c_c initialise the LSTM
hidden and cell state, c_sel conditions variable selection, and c_enrich conditions attention
enrichment. Without this wiring, industry and geography are just extra input columns and the entire
three-tier architecture stops doing anything. This is the single most important structural correction.

Reading the corrected flow
Internal telemetry and the sequence history X_t meet the industry mask m_ind at a gating junction
before the encoder — so industry-irrelevant features are suppressed at the input, not after
contamination. The Universal Encoder emits z_t as an explicit per-timestep sequence. Geography
contributes twice: a static latent e_geo into the fusion engine, and a time-varying known-future stream
g_t (scheduled rate decisions, holidays) into the encoder. The Fusion Engine broadcasts the static
signals across the sequence, then emits both the fused representation and a set of interpretability
weights. The Quantile Predictor produces Bear/Base/Bull.

3 · Component 1 — Universal Sequence Encoder
Tier 1. Consumes the time-series history of internal signals and produces the base feature trajectory
z_t — a per-timestep encoded representation of the company's recent state.
Inputs
X_t — the observed sequence: comms volume, after-hours ratio, sentiment, task throughput, PR/issue
velocity, doc-creation rate. Shape [T, F] (T timesteps, F features).
g_t — time-varying known-future geography signals (interest-rate path, holiday calendar).
Concatenated to X_t.
m_ind — the industry mask, applied element-wise to the input features BEFORE encoding.
Mechanism
A recurrent encoder (LSTM) processes the masked input sequence, followed by interpretable self-
attention across timesteps. The output is z_t of shape [T, d] where d is the hidden dimension.
z_t = Attention( LSTM( m_ind ⊙ [X_t ; g_t] ) )
⊙ = element-wise gate · [;] = concatenation · output shape [T, d]
W H Y M A S K AT I N P U T ( F I X 2 )
If the industry mask were applied after encoding, industry-irrelevant features would already be baked into
z_t. Masking the input means a finance company's encoder never even sees tech-specific signals — they
are zeroed before the LSTM. The representation is clean per industry.
4 · Component 2 — Industry Mask & Projector
Tier 2. Takes the industry type ID and produces a feature mask that gates which input signals are
relevant for that industry.
Mechanism
The industry ID indexes an embedding, which a small projector maps to a mask vector the same width as
the input features. A sigmoid keeps mask values in [0,1] — soft gating, so the model can learn partial
relevance rather than hard on/off.
m_ind = σ( W_m · Embed(industry) + b_m )
σ = sigmoid · values in [0,1] · one weight per input feature

5 · Component 3 — Geography (Static + Time-Varying)
Tier 3. The fix here is critical — geography is NOT purely static. It contributes both a static identity
latent and a time-varying known-future stream.
Static half — e_geo
The geo code (country) indexes an embedding projected to a latent vector that captures durable country
identity — "this is Kenya", carrying learned priors about that market.
e_geo = W_g · Embed(geo_code) + b_g
static per tenant · fused into every timestep by broadcast
Time-varying half — g_t (Fix 3)
Scheduled and observed country signals that change over time: interest-rate decision dates, inflation
trajectory, FX volatility, holidays, elections. These are known ahead for future timesteps and feed directly
into the encoder as known-future inputs.
W H Y G E O G R A P H Y N E E D S B O T H H A LV E S
A static embedding captures "this company is in Kenya" but not "Kenya's central bank meets next
Tuesday". A hiring forecast in a 12%-inflation, high-FX-volatility market must react to the trajectory of
those signals, not just the country label. The static latent gives identity; the time-varying stream gives
dynamics. Both are required.

6 · Component 4 — Dynamic Trajectory Fusion Engine
The convergence point. Combines three heterogeneous representations — the sequence trajectory
z_t, the static geo latent e_geo, and the industry context — into a single fused representation, and
emits interpretability weights.
Mechanism
The static signals (e_geo, industry context) are broadcast across every timestep of z_t, concatenated,
then passed through a gated feed-forward block with LayerNorm and GELU. This is where the "broadcast
fuse" happens — each timestep of the trajectory is contextualised by the company's static identity.
h_t = LayerNorm( z_t + GELU( W_f · [z_t ; e_geo ; c_ind] ) )
e_geo, c_ind broadcast across all T timesteps · h_t shape [T, d]
The interpretability head (Fix 5)
A parallel projection produces attention/selection weights indicating which inputs drove the forecast —
the "why" signal that becomes a competitive advantage in the product UI.
α = softmax( W_α · h_t )
α = per-feature importance weights, surfaced to the user
7 · Component 5 — Quantile Predictor
Takes the fused representation and projects it to three quantiles per future timestep, producing the
Bear / Base / Bull trajectory.
ŷ_τ(q) = W_q · h_T + b_q for q ∈ {0.1, 0.5, 0.9}
three linear heads, one per quantile, over the forecast horizon
QUANTILE 	CASE 	INTERPRETATION
P10 	Bear 	Pessimistic — only 10% chance the true value is below this
P50 	Base 	Median — the central forecast
P90 	Bull 	Optimistic — only 10% chance the true value is above this

9 · The Mathematics, Fully Explained
9.1 Quantile Loss — the training objective
The model is trained to minimise quantile loss (pinball loss). For a true value y, prediction ŷ at quantile q:
QL(y, ŷ, q) = q · max(y − ŷ, 0) + (1 − q) · max(ŷ − y, 0)
asymmetric penalty — controls which side of the error is punished harder
Intuition: for the P90 head (q=0.9), under-predicting (y > ŷ) is penalised 9× more than over-predicting.
This pushes the P90 line upward until it genuinely covers the high-scenario tail. The P10 head does the
opposite. This is what produces honest, asymmetric bands. The total loss sums over all quantiles and
timesteps:
L = Σ_q Σ_t QL( y_t, ŷ_t(q), q )
9.2 The Gated Linear Unit (GLU)
The gating mechanism throughout the fusion engine. It lets the network suppress irrelevant components
entirely by multiplying them toward zero — critical when a tenant has sparse data.
GLU(a) = σ(W₁ · a + b₁) ⊙ (W₂ · a + b₂)
σ = sigmoid gate · ⊙ = element-wise product
9.3 The Gated Residual Network (GRN)
The core reusable block. Wraps a nonlinear transform in a gate and a residual connection, so information
can flow through unchanged when the transform isn't needed.
GRN(a) = LayerNorm( a + GLU( W₃ · ELU(W₄ · a + b₄) + b₃ ) )
residual + gate = the network can learn to skip this block entirely
9.4 Interpretable Multi-Head Attention
How the encoder decides which past timesteps matter for the forecast. Standard scaled dot-product
attention, modified so value weights are shared across heads — which makes the resulting attention
weights directly interpretable as temporal importance.
Attention(Q, K, V) = softmax( Q Kᵀ / √d_k ) · V
the softmax weights reveal which historical weeks drove the prediction

Before fusion, the geo latent is L2-normalised so no single country embedding dominates by magnitude
— only its direction (learned identity) carries information.
ê_geo = e_geo / ||e_geo||₂
unit vector · stabilises fusion across countries of different data volumes

8 · Component 6 — Static Covariate Encoder
The conditioning engine. Takes the tenant's identity — industry, country, size band, currency — and
produces four context vectors that condition every other component. This is the mechanism by
which the three tiers actually influence the forecast.
Inputs
A dictionary of categorical IDs, one per static covariate. Each is embedded, the embeddings are
concatenated, and four separate Gated Residual Networks project the result into four purpose-specific
context vectors.
s = [ Embed(industry) ; Embed(country) ; Embed(size) ; Embed(currency) ]
concatenated static embedding, shape [B, d_in]
c_h = GRN₁(s) c_c = GRN₂(s) c_sel = GRN₃(s) c_enrich = GRN₄(s)
four independent projections, each shape [B, d_hidden]
What each context vector does
VECTOR 	CONSUMED BY 	EFFECT
c_h 	LSTM initial hidden state 	The sequence encoder starts from a tenant-specific state
c_c 	LSTM initial cell state 	Long-term memory is primed by tenant identity
c_sel 	Variable Selection Network 	Which features matter varies by industry and country
c_enrich 	Attention enrichment GRN 	Temporal attention is conditioned on identity
W H Y T H I S C O M P O N E N T I S LO A D - B E A R I N G
Remove this encoder and feed industry and country as ordinary input columns instead, and the model
treats them as two more numbers among fifty. The tier structure collapses into a flat feature soup. With
the encoder wired in, tenant identity reshapes the encoder's starting state, its feature selection, and its
attention — which is precisely what "conditioned by covariates rather than separate models per tenant"
means in practice.

10 · Feature Engineering — Events to Tensors
The hardest practical problem in this system: converting GitHub commits, Slack threads, Notion
documents, decisions, and task allocations into the numeric tensors the model consumes. This
section defines exactly how.
T H E C O R E P R I N C I P L E
You never convert an individual event into a number. An event has a timestamp; a time series needs a
value at every regular interval. The operation is therefore: aggregate events onto a fixed weekly
grid and compute features per bucket. A commit is not a data point — a week's worth of commit
behaviour is.
10.1 The five feature families
Family 1 — Counts (raw volume)
n_commits, n_prs_opened, n_prs_merged, n_messages, n_docs_created, n_docs_edited,
n_tasks_assigned, n_tasks_completed, n_decisions. One integer per signal per bucket.
Family 2 — Ratios (scale-free, often more predictive than counts)
after_hours_ratio, weekend_ratio, merge_rate (merged ÷ opened), completion_ratio (completed ÷
assigned), reopen_rate. These are already normalised by construction, which makes them transfer well
across tenants of different sizes.

Family 3 — Latencies (where timestamps become features)
median_review_delay (PR opened → first review), median_cycle_time (task assigned → completed),
median_response_delay (message → first reply), oldest_open_task_age, weeks_since_last_decision. Rising
cycle time is one of the strongest leading indicators of the delivery and burnout outcomes this system
forecasts.
Family 4 — Dispersion (how concentrated the work is)
Compute Shannon entropy over each person's share of the week's events:
H = − Σᵢ pᵢ log pᵢ
pᵢ = person i's share of that week's events
Low entropy means one person is carrying everything — a direct bus-factor and burnout signal. Also:
top_actor_share and n_active_actors.
Family 5 — Text-derived (Slack and Notion content reduced to scalars)
sentiment_mean and sentiment_std from a small classifier over the week's messages, plus topic_drift —
the cosine distance between this week's mean embedding and a rolling eight-week baseline centroid.
Because the embeddings already exist in Qdrant for retrieval, topic drift is nearly free to compute and
directly powers the topic-shift forecast.
D O N O T F E E D R AW E M B E D D I N G S A S VA R I A B L E S
Passing a 384-dimension embedding into the model as 384 separate time-varying variables is a mistake.
Variable selection treats each variable independently, so the network becomes expensive to train and
impossible to interpret. Reduce text to a handful of meaningful scalars instead.
10.2 What must NOT be a static covariate
author_id , repo_id , and channel_id are not static covariates. Static means constant across the entire
series for that entity — but the set of authors changes every week as people join, leave, and rotate.
These fields are also high-cardinality and do not transfer across tenants: repo 7 in tenant A has nothing
in common with repo 7 in tenant B, so a shared embedding over them is meaningless. Replace them
entirely with the aggregate Family 4 dispersion features.
10.3 Bucket size
Weekly, matching the 13-week horizon. Hourly or 15-minute bucketing is a common instinct and is wrong
here: it produces thousands of near-empty, extremely noisy timesteps, and the outcomes being forecast
— burnout, velocity, knowledge gaps — simply do not resolve at that granularity. Daily is the finest
defensible bucket, and only if the tenant volume is high enough to justify it.

11 · Normalisation — The Six Rules
This is where multi-tenant forecasting implementations most often fail silently. Each rule below
prevents a specific, real failure mode. Rules 1 and 6 are the two that would quietly destroy the
model without producing any visible error.
Rule 1 — Normalise per tenant, not globally
A ten-person startup produces around 50 commits a week; a 500-person company produces 5,000. Feed
raw counts into a global model and it spends its capacity learning company size instead of organisational
dynamics — and the cross-tenant learning that constitutes the entire competitive moat stops working. Z-
score every feature against that tenant's own history, or convert to per-active-contributor rates.
Rule 2 — Log-transform counts before scaling
Event counts are heavy-tailed: a single release week can be ten times a normal week. Apply log1p(x)
first, then z-score. Without this, a handful of outlier weeks dominate the scaling and compress all normal
variation toward zero.
Rule 3 — Encode calendar time cyclically
Week 52 and week 1 are adjacent in reality but maximally distant as integers. Encode as a sine and
cosine pair so the model sees the wrap-around correctly.
week_sin = sin(2πw / 52) week_cos = cos(2πw / 52)
always used as a pair — one alone is ambiguous
Rule 4 — Categoricals become embeddings, never raw integers
Feeding industry_id = 7 into a numeric channel implies industry 7 is "greater than" industry 3, which is
meaningless. Every categorical — industry, country, size band, currency — goes through nn.Embedding .
Rule 5 — Missing buckets are explicit, never silently zero-filled
Zero means "no activity happened". Missing means "we did not observe". These are different facts and
the model must be able to tell them apart. Emit two additional channels:
CHANNEL 	MEANING
observed 	Binary mask — 1 if the week had any data at all
weeks_since_last_event 	Run-length of consecutive unobserved weeks
Carry-forward of continuous states has the same defect: it manufactures data that was never measured,
and the model learns staleness as though it were signal.

Rule 6 — Fit normalisation statistics on the training window only
If the mean and standard deviation used for z-scoring are computed over a range that includes future
weeks, the future has leaked backwards into the past. The backtest will look excellent and the production
forecasts will be worthless. Fit on the training window, then apply those frozen statistics forward to
validation and inference.
T H E S I L E N T FA I LU R E
Rules 1 and 6 both fail without raising an error. The model trains, the loss decreases, the validation
metrics look strong — and the deployed forecasts are meaningless. Assert both in tests: verify that
normalisation statistics are tenant-scoped, and that no statistic was computed from a timestamp later
than the training cut-off.

12.1 Industry Mask & Projector
import torch, torch.nn as nn
class IndustryMask(nn.Module):
# Tier 2 — produces a soft feature mask from industry ID
def __init__(self, n_industries, n_features, emb=16):
super().__init__()
self.embed = nn.Embedding(n_industries, emb)
self.proj = nn.Linear(emb, n_features)
def forward(self, industry_id):
e = self.embed(industry_id)
return torch.sigmoid(self.proj(e)) # [B, n_features] in [0,1]
12.2 Universal Sequence Encoder (mask applied at input — Fix 2)
class UniversalEncoder(nn.Module):
def __init__(self, n_features, hidden=64):
super().__init__()
self.lstm = nn.LSTM(n_features, hidden, batch_first=True)
self.attn = nn.MultiheadAttention(hidden, num_heads=4, batch_first=True)
def forward(self, x_seq, g_seq, m_ind):
# x_seq [B,T,F] observed · g_seq [B,T,G] known-future · m_ind [B,F]
x = torch.cat([x_seq, g_seq], dim=-1)
m = torch.cat([m_ind, torch.ones_like(g_seq[:, 0, :])], dim=-1)
x = x * m.unsqueeze(1) 	# GATE AT INPUT
z, _ = self.lstm(x) 	# [B,T,d]
z, attn_w = self.attn(z, z, z) 	# interpretable attn
return z, attn_w 	# z_t is a SEQUENCE (Fix 1)
12.3 Geography Projector (static latent — Fix 3 time-varying feeds encoder)
class GeoProjector(nn.Module):
def __init__(self, n_regions, latent=32, emb=16):
super().__init__()
self.embed = nn.Embedding(n_regions, emb)
self.proj = nn.Linear(emb, latent)
def forward(self, geo_code):
e = self.proj(self.embed(geo_code))
return nn.functional.normalize(e, dim=-1) # L2 norm

12.4 Fusion Engine + Quantile Predictor
class FusionEngine(nn.Module):
def __init__(self, d, geo_dim, ind_dim):
super().__init__()
self.ff = nn.Linear(d + geo_dim + ind_dim, d)
self.norm = nn.LayerNorm(d)
self.act = nn.GELU()
self.interp = nn.Linear(d, d) 	# interpretability head (Fix 5)
def forward(self, z, e_geo, c_ind):
T = z.size(1)
eg = e_geo.unsqueeze(1).expand(-1, T, -1) # broadcast
ci = c_ind.unsqueeze(1).expand(-1, T, -1)
h = self.act(self.ff(torch.cat([z, eg, ci], dim=-1)))
h = self.norm(z + h)
alpha = torch.softmax(self.interp(h), dim=-1)
return h, alpha
class QuantilePredictor(nn.Module):
def __init__(self, d, horizon, quantiles=(0.1, 0.5, 0.9)):
super().__init__()
self.heads = nn.ModuleList([nn.Linear(d, horizon) for _ in quantiles])
self.quantiles = quantiles
def forward(self, h):
last = h[:, -1, :] 	# [B,d]
return {q: head(last) for q, head in zip(self.quantiles, self.heads)}
12.5 Quantile Loss
def quantile_loss(preds, target):
# preds: dict{q: [B,H]}, target: [B,H]
total = 0.0
for q, yhat in preds.items():
err = target - yhat
total = total + torch.max(q * err, (q - 1) * err).mean()
return total
T H I S I S T H E E X E C U TA B L E F O R M O F YO U R D I A G R A M
Each class is one box in the corrected architecture. Wire them in order: IndustryMask → UniversalEncoder
(masked input) → GeoProjector → FusionEngine → QuantilePredictor, trained with quantile_loss. Swap the
LSTM for the full pytorch-forecasting TFT when you scale — the interfaces match.

row["median_cycle_time"] = _median_meta(g, "cycle_time_s")
# Family 4 — dispersion
shares = g["actor"].value_counts(normalize=True).values
row["actor_entropy"] = float(-(shares * np.log(shares + 1e-9)).sum())
row["top_actor_share"] = float(shares.max()) if len(shares) else 0.0
row["n_active_actors"] = int(g["actor"].nunique())
rows.append(row)
df = pd.DataFrame(rows).set_index("week").sort_index()
# Rule 5 — complete the grid so gaps become EXPLICIT
full = pd.date_range(df.index.min(), df.index.max(), freq="W-MON")
df = df.reindex(full)
df["observed"] = df["observed"].fillna(0).astype(int)
gap = df["observed"].eq(0)
df["weeks_since_last_event"] = gap.groupby((~gap).cumsum()).cumsum()
df["tenant_id"] = tenant_id
return df

13.2 Topic drift from existing embeddings
def weekly_text_features(week_embeddings, baseline_centroid, sentiments):
out = {"sentiment_mean": 0.0, "sentiment_std": 0.0, "topic_drift": 0.0}
if sentiments:
out["sentiment_mean"] = float(np.mean(sentiments))
out["sentiment_std"] = float(np.std(sentiments))
if week_embeddings is not None and len(week_embeddings) \
and baseline_centroid is not None:
c = week_embeddings.mean(axis=0)
c = c / (np.linalg.norm(c) + 1e-9)
b = baseline_centroid / (np.linalg.norm(baseline_centroid) + 1e-9)
out["topic_drift"] = float(1.0 - np.dot(c, b)) # cosine distance
return out
13.3 Per-tenant normaliser (Rules 1, 2, 3, 6)
class TenantNormalizer:
"""Per tenant. Fit on the TRAINING WINDOW ONLY."""
def __init__(self):
self.stats = {}
def fit(self, df, train_end):
train = df.loc[:train_end].copy()
for c in COUNT_COLS: 	# Rule 2
if c in train: train[c] = np.log1p(train[c].fillna(0))
for c in train.select_dtypes("number").columns:
if c in PASSTHROUGH: continue
self.stats[c] = (float(train[c].mean()), float(train[c].std()))
return self
def transform(self, df):
out = df.copy()
for c in COUNT_COLS:
if c in out: out[c] = np.log1p(out[c].fillna(0))
for c, (mu, sd) in self.stats.items(): # Rule 1 + 6
if c in out: out[c] = (out[c] - mu) / (sd + 1e-6)
w = pd.DatetimeIndex(out.index).isocalendar().week.values.astype(float)
out["week_sin"] = np.sin(2 * np.pi * w / 52.0) 	# Rule 3
out["week_cos"] = np.cos(2 * np.pi * w / 52.0)
num = out.select_dtypes("number").columns
out[num] = out[num].fillna(0.0)
return out
13.4 Rolling-window self-labelling
def make_windows(arr, encoder_len, horizon):
"""The future values ARE the labels — every elapsed week turns a
prediction into a labelled training example."""
T = arr.shape[0]
last_start = T - (encoder_len + horizon)
for s in range(0, max(last_start + 1, 0)):
x = arr[s : s + encoder_len]
y = arr[s + encoder_len : s + encoder_len + horizon]
yield x, y

14.4 Labels — the hard part
Forecasting is supervised: you need the future outcome to train against. Your labels come from your own
historical data via a rolling-window setup: train on weeks 1–52, predict week 53–65, and the actual
values of those weeks (once they arrive) are the labels. This means the system improves automatically
as time passes — every week that elapses turns a prediction into a labelled training example.
T H E D ATA F LY W H E E L
Internal signals are self-labelling over time. Each forecast you make becomes a training example once the
real outcome arrives. Combined with cross-tenant learning, this means the system gets more accurate
the longer it runs and the more tenants you add — a compounding data advantage a single-company tool
can never replicate.

15 · Tenant Isolation & Cold-Start
Two operational concerns the architecture must handle explicitly: keeping tenants cryptographically
separate, and producing useful forecasts for brand-new tenants with no history.
15.1 Tenant isolation
Every store in the architecture is namespaced per tenant. The single global model is shared (that is the
source of cross-tenant learning), but no raw data, sequence trajectory, or forecast ever crosses a tenant
boundary.
LAYER 	ISOLATION MECHANISM
Sequence store (LMDB) 	Per-tenant encrypted database, separate keys
Feature store 	tenant_id namespace prefix on every record
Forecast store 	Namespace-scoped queries, RBAC enforced
Model 	Shared weights only — learns patterns, never memorises raw tenant data
Serving API 	User's tenant_id must match requested namespace
S H A R E D M O D E L , I S O L AT E D D ATA — H O W B O T H H O L D
The model learns generalisable patterns ("rising after-hours activity precedes attrition") across the fleet,
expressed in its weights. It never stores a tenant's raw sequence. So two competing finance companies
benefit from the same learned dynamics while their actual data remains cryptographically separate. This
is the balance that makes multi-tenant forecasting both powerful and safe.
15.2 Cold-start — forecasting for a new tenant
A brand-new tenant has almost no X_t history. The architecture handles this gracefully because identity
comes from static conditioning, not just history.
Lean on static priors. With little sequence data, the fusion engine weights the industry and
geography signals more heavily — the model forecasts "what a typical finance company in Kenya
looks like" until real data refines it.
Widen the bands. Low data means high uncertainty, and the quantile output should reflect that
honestly — the Bear–Bull spread widens automatically when the model is uncertain, because the
quantile loss learns wider spreads where errors are larger.
Fleet-based warm start. Because the model is global, a new tenant inherits everything learned
from similar tenants (same industry, same region) from day one.
Narrow over time. As the tenant's own history accumulates via the rolling window, the encoder's
sequence signal grows stronger and the bands tighten.
